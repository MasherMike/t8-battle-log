Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR) in neutral. The TekkenDocs sidestep chart (community chart by @Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) says SSR for Clive. TekkenDocs has no anti-strat page for him yet. Wavu and TekkenDocs carry only one per-move step note for Clive, so the default rests on the chart.

Exceptions:
• f+2,2,H.1* (Heat only, held, +11 on block, Guard Break): Wavu — "Whiffs vs SSL, SW from 2nd block" and "Whiffs vs SSR, SWR from 2nd hit." After you block f+2,2 in his Heat, step left, not right. This is the one place the per-move note and the chart point different ways, and the note wins for that move.
• Homing — don't step: df+3 (low, −26), db+1 and db+1,1 (high opener), b+1 (mid, −13), ws3 (mid), PHX.3+4 (mid, +7), and both generic throws. Wavu also marks db+4 (low) and b+2 (high) "Effectively homing?", with the question mark.
• Phoenix Shift (PHX): his stance attacks out of a plus entry come too fast to step on reaction (see Stance Check). Block, don't step.

So the habit is: SSR his neutral, step left only after a blocked f+2,2 in Heat, and don't step anything homing. Most of his homing moves are punishable on block, so blocking them costs you nothing.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. No source says Clive can block in PHX, WOL or GAR. For PHX, Wavu's f+4 entry says "Block at frame 35 (i35 standing guard)", which is when he gets his guard back, not a block inside the stance. Clive also has a ZAN gauge: 1+4 / PHX.1+4 grow in damage with it (10 → 75) and become an attack throw on hit at ZAN2~5.

General Counter Play↑ Return to Top

Practice priorities

1. Don't jab after his +6 PHX entries (4,4, df+1,2, and f,f,F+2 at +8). PHX.2 and the PHX throw come out effectively i9, PHX.1 absorbs the jab, and PHX.4 ducks under it. Block, and break the throw with 1+2.
2. Duck his plus highs: the 1 of f+2,1 (+1), the 4 of 4,4 and the 2 of df+1,2 (the two +6 PHX cancels), b+2 (+4 PHX), qcf+1 (hit-throw).
3. Launch the big ones: lows df+3 and PHX.3 (−26), db+4 (−18), d+3 (−17), d+4 (−15); mids uf+2 (−17), 3,3 (−18~−16), df+1,2,1 and SS.2,1 (−15), WOL.2 (−38~−23).
4. SSR in neutral; step left only after a blocked f+2,2 in Heat; don't step homing moves.
5. Deny Heat by punishing, not ducking: f+1+2 (−12), f+2,2,2 (−13), SS.2,1 (−15).

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus PHX entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a startup range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered; where Wavu gives a separate "Enter PHX" number, that number is used instead of the move's block value. Airborne (a) and knockdown (d) hit advantages are left out, so f,f,F+2's +43d hit and db+1+2,1's "+41a (+23) GAR on airborne hit" aren't tabled.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. No source says Clive can block in PHX.
WOL and GAR don't apply: WOL's only stance entries (3,3 and df+1,4,3) are −18~−16 on block with an airborne hit advantage, and GAR's only stated advantage is airborne.
State changes the read (Wavu/TekkenDocs): at +N your jab lands on his frame 10+N. PHX.1 is a power crush 7~20, so it absorbs the jab at every on-block entry below (frame 14 at +4, 16 at +6, 18 at +8). A standing jab is a high, so it whiffs against PHX.4 (crouching 6~47). PHX.3+4 is airborne 18~39, which beats lows, not a high jab. Wavu: "Stance moves can be delayed by 9F", and a delayed move is slower than the table shows.
