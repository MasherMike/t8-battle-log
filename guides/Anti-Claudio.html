Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep LEFT (SSL) against his strings, once you've blocked the first hit. The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written) says "Generally SSL for b3, uf4, ... SWL for df1. Has decent tracking, may have to walk to evade (lows track well)." Wavu's firm whiff notes point the same way: d+1,2 and ws1,2 both whiff vs SSL / SWL from 1st block, and so do their Starburst versions (d+1,STB.2, ws1,STB.2, which are +6 on block, so stepping them matters more). uf+3 has "no tracking to SSL/SWL side?". The @Mezerdie chart also says SSL.

Exceptions — his quick pokes track, so don't step them:
• Tracks your left step: 4 (pseudo homing to SSL/SWL?), f+2 (homing to SSL/SWL?), df+2 (completely homing to SSL/SWL?), db+4 (pseudo homing to SSL/SWL?).
• Tracks your right step: 3 (tracks SSR/SWR completely?), df+3 (homing to SSR/SWR?), df+1 (can't be stepped or walked right?), f+3+4 (good right sidestep tracking).
• b+3: Wavu says "Completely homing to SSL/SSR/SWR?" — that disagrees with the anti-strat's "SSL for b3". Following Wavu (Tier 1): SSL won't beat it; only a left sidewalk is left off Wavu's list. It's −4, so just block it.
• Fully homing: 4,3, f+3, b+1, b+4,3, b+3+4 and both generic throws (list below).

So the habit is: block his first poke, then SSL the follow-up of d+1 and ws1. Against the charged f,F+3,2* either side works (SSR on a short charge).

Duck and Launch↑ Return to Top

Most of his duck chances are mid-high string enders (3,2, b+4,1, b+4,3, df+3,2, ws1,1, f,F+2,2) — ducking them is a read, and several are combos from the 1st hit. His two full-high strings are 1,2 and 4,3. Not duckable: the arrow in 4,STB.2 is a special mid. His power crush b+1+2 is a mid, so don't duck it.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Starburst (STB) is a power-up, not a posture. He doesn't enter a crouch or back-turned stance: the STB moves are his normal inputs (d+1,2, ws1,2, b+4,3,2, f+1+2,1+2…) with better frames, and using one consumes STB. Anti-strat: "Moves get improved frames and properties." So there's no "can he block in stance" question here — the question is which of your punishes disappear.

STB — Starburst

How he gets it (Wavu): on hit from 2,1,2, 3,2, b+4,2, uf+2, f,f,F+2, f+2,2, ws1,1, ws2, f,F+2,1+2, ub+2, uf+3+4,2 and 1+2,1+2,2; on counter hit from df+2; on hit or block from the fully charged f,F+3,2*. Many of these are Heat Engagers too — getting hit hands him both.
In Heat: anti-strat — "Free Starburst moves. Retains STB after heat." Wavu lists each STB move as "Consumes STB | Consumes 240F of remaining Heat time" (600F for f+1+2,STB.1+2, 300F for STB.wr1+2).
Lows: none — every STB move is a mid or high, so stand-block when he has it. Fastest STB move: STB.wr1+2 i13~16 (high, +8g~+11g). No power crush or parry among the STB moves.

Stage 1 (direction only, timing untested): dummy plays d+1 on fixed timing (the opener of d+1,2); after the block, SSL. 20-rep sets, graduate at 18/20 on 3 straight sets. Stage 2: the real d+1,2 and ws1,2 on random playback, 16/20 on 3 straight sets. Stage 3: mix in d+1 alone (−8, take your turn), ws1,1 (high 2nd hit: duck it instead) and the STB versions (+6 if blocked), 25-rep sets, 75% correct decisions on 2 straight sets. Stage 4: log what holds in real matches as Lab (Mike). Why Stage 3 matters for him: the same ws1 opener leads to a high (duck) or a mid (step left), so the step must become a read. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock and recovers differently from the same hit inside the string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Cap a session around 100–150 reps; stop if accuracy drops 15 points from where you started; go back a stage if you're under 60% for two sets. Rep counts are a rule of thumb, not research.
