Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written) opens with one word: "SSR". The @Mezerdie sidestep chart (via sidestep.md) also says SSR. Panda's Wavu pages carry no per-move whiff or step notes — only homing tags — so nothing contradicts SSR, and nothing confirms it move by move either.

Exceptions:
• Charge moves (db+1,2, ws1,2, HBS.1,2 held): anti-strat — "Full charge extremely linear, SS both ways. Medium charge: SSL seems the most consistent for all options. Smaller charge might require SWL. Tiny delay will hit everything." Against a held charge, step LEFT, not right. The anti-strat also says punishing the first hit (db+1 -10, ws1 -11, HBS.1 -13 on Wavu) "will also beat medium and max hold."
• Four attacks are homing (list below), and both generic throws home too. f+1+2 is tagged "Pseudo-homing?" by Wavu.

So the habit is: SSR in neutral; SSL when you see her hold a charge; don't step the homing list.

Standing Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Three traps. (1) Bait stops / extensions: 1,1 is -15 but 1,1,1 -16; uf+3 -13, uf+3,4 -11, uf+3,4,1+2 -15; b+2,2 -10 then b+2,2,1 -15; df+1,2 -11 then df+1,2,1+2 -14c. Confirm the string ended. (2) Charge levels: db+1,2, ws1,2 and HBS.1,2 are -13 uncharged, -12 half-charged, but +3 fully charged (**). (3) Stance cancels: many strings cancel to HBS with D or 3+4; Wavu gives a block frame for only a few (b+1~D -13, b+2,1~D -4, b+2,1~F -14 into ROL). Anti-strat flags pushback on f+3+4,1+2, b+3+4, f,F+2, HBS.1+2 and HBS.df+2 — use a long-range punisher.

Panda vs Kuma (Wavu, both pages): same core moveset, but Panda has no 10-hit string and no H.df+2+3; she has H.uf+3+4 (+12g SIT) and the unbreakable H.db+1+3 instead. Practice first

1. Low drill: dummy randomly does db+4, f+3+4, ub+1+2 and qcf+2,2 — block on reaction and launch (all -17 or worse). 2. Block 1+2 and punish -13; duck f+2 / f+2,1 and launch. 3. Charge read: block db+1, punish it, or SSL a medium charge. 4. Block H.2+3 and hold — she's +9, or +13 in HBS. 5. Check HBS with a fast mid, not a standing jab.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt her plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = her raw startup − her frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. No source says Panda can block in HBS, ROL or SIT — but Wavu says she can low parry in HBS with F.
State changes the read: a standing jab is a high, so it whiffs against HBS.2 (crouching 1~ on Wavu). HBS.3+4 is a power crush (7~19) and absorbs the jab. No other Panda stance move has a state listed on Wavu.

Lab drill — training wheels: (1) Stage 1 — dummy throws a standalone db+1; block it, then SSL right away, 20-rep sets, until 18/20 on three straight sets (direction only, timing untested). Stage 2 — the real db+1,2* (medium charge) with randomized hold length, one charge level at a time. Stage 3 — mix db+1 alone (-10, punish), db+1,2 uncharged (-13, block and punish), medium charge (SSL) and full charge (+3, either side) — and try the anti-strat's answer, punishing the first hit, against all of them. Panda's charge strings put the read on hold length, so Stage 3 is where the real decision lives. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
