Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR) against his neutral pokes. Set by Tier 2, the TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written): "SSR in general, SWL against stance and df1." Tier 1 agrees on the one standing poke it covers: Wavu (and TekkenDocs) say f+2 "Has good left sidestep tracking", so a left step is the wrong habit against it. Tier disagreement: the TekkenDocs sidestep chart (@Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) says SSL for Leroy. The anti-strat page exists and is more specific, and Wavu's f+2 note points the same way, so the guide follows SSR.

Exceptions:
• Hermit (HRM) is a different question. The anti-strat says SWL against the stance. Wavu's counterplay page agrees for HRM.2,3 ("will catch steps to the right": duck, or SSL/SWL, SWL better), but says HRM.b+3,1+2 catches SSL, SSR and SWL (answer: duck or SWR). HRM.f+1, HRM.4,1+2 and HRM.1+2,1 are all listed "to catch steps", and HRM.b+4 is homing. Tier 1 wins on each named move: in HRM, stepping is a read on one move, not a default.
• df+1 (mid, i13~14, −6): the anti-strat says SWL. No Wavu note on it.
• Homing (Wavu), don't step: b+3 (high, i22~23, +9~+10 into HRM on block: duck it), b+4 (mid), HRM.b+4 (low, high crush 8~37), db+2+3 (cane low, "Tracks to both sides effectively homing", once per match) and his 1+3 / 2+4 throws.

So the habit is: SSR his neutral pokes; duck b+3; against Hermit, block first and step only on a read (SWL for HRM.2,3, SWR for the HRM.b+3 follow-up).

Nothing in this section is lab-tested.
Theory (Ledger): The anti-strat's SWL vs df+1 is most likely aimed at its follow-ups: df+1,4 (mid, −9 into HRM) and df+1,1+2 (mid, then highs; combo from 1st hit, so on block the highs can also be ducked). df+1 itself is −6, so the step is only worth it after you block the first hit. Test: dummy plays df+1,4 and df+1,1+2,2 after a blocked df+1; does SWL whiff the 2nd hit of both?

Homing — don't step these at all

b+3 (high, i22~23, +9~+10 into HRM on block, Balcony Break) · b+4 (mid, i17~18, −9) · HRM.b+4 (low, i20~21, −13, high crush 8~37) · db+2+3 (cane: low, mid; −14; once per match) · 1+3, 2+4 (throws). All Wavu. b+3 is the big one: a slow homing high that leaves him plus in Hermit. Duck it and launch the whiff.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Leroy has one real stance, HRM (Hermit), plus sidestep moves (SS) reached from it with HRM.d (Nimble Shift). The plus entries and the jab arithmetic are in Stance Check. Can he block in Hermit? No source says he can. What he has instead is defence built into the stance: Wavu says HRM is an "Auto Strong low parry" from frame 3, HRM.P is a low parry, HRM.b+2 is a parry on frames 3~15, and HRM.1 is a sabaki on frames 3~12 that parries high or mid punches or kicks. The anti-strat sums it up: "Generally not good transitions on block, either punishable or he has to parry to get out. A few exceptions (b3 and HB). On hit he can always frame trap. SWL is a good option. Stance has 'everything'. Manual HRM cancel (3+4) vulnerable for 5f."

General Counter Play↑ Return to Top

Practice priorities

1. Duck b+3. It's a homing high, i22~23, and +9~+10 into Hermit on block (Wavu). Stepping doesn't work; ducking does, and the whiff is yours to launch.
2. Launch the chain punches, but wait for the last hit. 1+2,1+2 is −2; 1+2,1+2,1 and HRM.1+2,1 are −17 (both Heat Engagers). df+2,1+2 is −18.
3. Never check Hermit with a low. The stance auto-parries lows from frame 3. Stand-block it, and launch HRM.b+3 (−26) when you guess low.
4. Block f+1+2 clean. It's a mid power crush: −13 if you just block it, −9 if your button got absorbed.
5. SSR his neutral pokes. f+2 tracks left steps (Wavu); the anti-strat says SSR in general.
