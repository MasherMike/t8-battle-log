Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR) up close. The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written): "Can't step b1 anyway, SSR better close (df1, d2, d1, 2)." Wavu's per-move whiff notes lean the same way, narrowly: three Steve follow-ups whiff vs SSR/SWR, two whiff vs SSL/SWL, two whiff either way. The @Mezerdie sidestep chart (via sidestep.md) says SSL — that is the one disagreement, and the higher tiers win (see the evidence note below).

Exceptions:
• b+1 (i13 high, his main counter-hit tool): anti-strat says you can't step it. Duck it or high-crush it instead (Wavu counterplay: "using high crushing moves to slip under b1 is also recommended").
• Against Peekaboo (PAB): anti-strat — "SSL block is good against PAB. SSL duck more greedy." But Wavu says PAB.df+1,1,2 whiffs vs SSR from 2nd block, and PAB.2 / PAB.df+1,2 are homing. So: SSL vs PAB generally, SSR only for the PAB.df+1,1~2 follow-up.
• Against Flicker (FLK): anti-strat — "SSR block is good against FLK."
• Weave Swindlers (LWV.1,2~1, RWV.1,2~1) whiff to the left (Wavu), not right.
• Ten moves are homing (list below) — most of his plus-on-block LNH enders.

So the habit is: SSR in close-range neutral and after df+1; SSL when he sits in Peekaboo; duck b+1; don't step the homing list.

Evidence tier: the SSR default is set by Tier 2 — the TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview ("SSR better close", community-written). Standing Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Three traps. (1) Stance escapes: many punishable strings can cancel into a stance for much better frames — qcf+1 -10 but -3 into FLK with B; LWV.1 -10 but -3 into FLK; PAB.b+1,1,2,1 -10 but -3 into FLK; df+2 -11 but -7 / -3 into weave / DCK; b+1 -14 but -1 into FLK. Watch for the stance before you commit. (2) Bait stops: 1,2,1 is -3 but 1,2,1,2 -10; 2,1 -5 but 2,1,2 -11; f+1+2 -14 but f+1+2,2 -13; LWV.1 -10 then LWV.1,1 -13 — and the ~1 / ~2 psych-out cancels change both the frames and the hit level. (3) Pushback (anti-strat): FC.df+2 and PAB.uf+2 are "-15~-14, almost always -15" with pushback, SWY.2 -18 — use a long-range punisher.

Low Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Reactability tag (Mike's reaction rule, using the low's fastest startup frame): Reactable 25f or slower · Mostly reactable 21–24f · Not reactable — guess or condition 20f or faster. Startups are from the Wavu movelist (for a string's low hit, Wavu's startup for that hit); a low with no stated startup gets no tag.
Anti-strat: "weak lows." Wavu counterplay: "Steve's lows are mostly pokes (d1, d2,1) and db3,2 (which forces him into LH stance) only works at point blank range." The fast lows (d+1, d+2, db+3, qcf+2) are not reactable but most are -13 or worse; d+2 and qcf+2 are -18. His slow lows (db+2, the low ends of 1,2,1 and b+1) are reactable and -11 to -12. Steve's highs are the matchup. His counter-hit poke (b+1), his plus LNH enders, his guard break (LNH.1+2) and his Flicker power crush (FLK.b+2) are all highs. His power crush in neutral (db+1+2) is a mid — see Power Crush.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Steve has nine stances on Wavu. The four that matter for blocking are Lionheart (LNH), Peekaboo (PAB), Flicker (FLK) and the weaves/ducks (LWV, RWV, SWY, DCK, EXD). Albatross (ALB) and the Heat-only Two-Faced (TFA) are smaller.

LNH — Lionheart

Entries (Wavu): ub+3 directly. Plus — H.2+3 (Heat Smash) +8 on block; ub+2, PAB.2, DCK.f+2, EXD.f+2 +5; b+1,2, LWV.1,2, RWV.1,2, PAB.df+1,2 +3. Minus — f+2,2, ws2,2, db+3,2, uf+3,2 -6 (+8 to +11 on hit).
Can he block? Anti-strat: "Can't block, has to cancel into sway." Wavu lists the cancels: 3 / 4 to weave, f+3_4 to DCK, b+3_4 to SWY, b to standing (adds 4 frames of recovery on the enders: +3 → -1).
Lows: none. Fastest: LNH.1 i14~15 mid, homing (-9). Guard break: LNH.1+2 i18 high (+12). Heat: an auto parry for highs and mids (H.LNH.P; anti-strat: "not elbows, airborne, knees").
So: after a +3 or +5 LNH entry, he has one fast mid (LNH.1) and one slow high (LNH.1+2). Stand-block, and duck on a read of the guard break.

General Counter Play↑ Return to Top

Wavu counterplay — General Advice: "Steve's main gameplan is to figure out his opponents rhythm and use his strong counter hit moves (b1, df2) in order to get a lot of damage. As the opponent, you would want to avoid having predictable timing and also try to force steve approach you, because Steve's lows are mostly pokes (d1, d2,1) and db3,2 (which forces him into LH stance) only works at point blank range."
Anti-strat Quick overview: "CH character, weak lows." "Good frames on transitions into stances."

Practice first

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered; Wavu writes stance transitions as (block / hit / CH).
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. For Steve, sources do say otherwise in two places: Wavu gives PAB an "auto high/mid block" and FLK an "i16 standing block from manual transition". The anti-strat says he can't block in LNH (he must cancel to sway). No source says he can block in SWY, DCK or ALB.
State changes the read: FLK.b+2 is a power crush (absorbs a jab); PAB.2 parries punches (a jab gets parried); FLK.u+2 has jab evasion; PAB.uf+2 is airborne from frame 9; SWY.2 and the whole DCK stance are crouching (a standing jab whiffs).

Lab drill — training wheels: Stage 1 — dummy throws a standalone df+1; block it, then SSR right away, 20-rep sets, until 18/20 on three straight sets (direction only, timing untested). Stage 2 — the real df+1,2~2 (the mid that whiffs vs SSR/SWR on Wavu) with randomized delay on the cancel. Stage 3 — mix df+1 alone (-2, your turn is small), df+1,2 (high — duck), df+1,2~1 (high, whiffs either way), df+1,2~2 (mid — SSR) and a throw, randomized. Steve's psych-out cancels change the hit level after the same first hit, so Stage 3 is where the real decision lives. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
