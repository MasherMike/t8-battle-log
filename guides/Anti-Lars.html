Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR) in neutral. The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written) says "SSR in neutral (df1, 2,1, db1, df2, ...)" — his most-used pokes. The @Mezerdie sidestep chart (via sidestep.md) also says SSR. Wavu's per-move notes don't name a default but don't contradict it.

Exceptions:
• uf+1 (i15 mid, 2 hits): Wavu — "Has good right sidestep tracking." It catches your SSR. It's −12 on block, so block it and punish.
• b+2 (i15 mid): Wavu — "Has good left sidestep tracking." It catches an SSL, which is one more reason to stay with SSR.
• db+2,3* (held, +13 on block): Wavu — "Whiffs vs SSL, SW from 1st block" and "vs SS, SW from 1st hit". Against the held version step LEFT (or walk either way); the gap is i36 from 1st block.
• Homing (list below): b+1, b+1+2, DEN.3, SEN.4 and both generic throws. Wavu also marks f,f,F+1 "Effectively homing?" (unconfirmed).

So the habit is: SSR his neutral pokes; block uf+1 and the homing moves; SSL or walk when he holds db+2,3. Against stance transitions the anti-strat says the answer is interrupting, not stepping — see Stance Counterplay.

Block values are from Wavu's Lars counterplay tables and movelist; all 119 moves on both sites match TekkenDocs.

Three traps. (1) Stance cancels: many of his punishable moves can cancel into a stance at better frames — 3 is −17 but −11 into LEN with D; ws2,3 is −15 but −11 into LEN; b+3 is −12 but −5 into SEN with F; f+1,2,3 is −5 into DEN (−12 only if he cancels with B). Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Lars has three stances, and the anti-strat sums up the matchup: "Stance character. Have to interrupt stance transitions." DEN (Dynamic Entry) is entered with f+3; SEN (Silent Entry) with DEN.n,F or many ~F cancels; LEN (Limited Entry) with SEN.D or ~D cancels. He can chain DEN→SEN, SEN→DEN (B) and SEN→LEN (D). Wavu doesn't say he can block in any of them — Mike's rule applies: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. Anti-strat: "For SEN, and minus transitions, you can df1 check and still duck the PC. From heat f3+4 and DEN3 you have to hold the mixup. For DEN and LEN, just play according to transition frames." (Wavu lists SEN's power crush, SEN.1+2, as a mid, so it can't be ducked — see the SEN note.)

General Counter Play↑ Return to Top

Practice priorities

1. SSR his neutral pokes (df+1, 2,1, db+1, df+2) — but block uf+1 (tracks SSR, −12) and the homing moves.
2. Watch the stance cancel before you punish: 3 −17 or −11 into LEN · ws2,3 −15 or −11 into LEN · b+3 −12 or −5 into SEN · f+1,2,3 −5 into DEN.
3. Respect his plus SEN entries (DEN.3 +5, H.f+3+4 +5, f,f,F+1 +4): SEN.1 is effectively i7 — block. After his minus SEN entries, check him.
4. Duck f,f,F+1 (+4 high), the high enders of b+3,4 and ws2,3, and stay crouched after d+3.
5. Launch his big lows: f,F+4 −31, db+4 −26, d+1+2 −20, f,F+3+4 −16.
