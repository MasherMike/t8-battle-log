Bryan on P1 side — inputs face right

Movement — Which Way to Step

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). Wavu's per-move notes only ever name the right side when they pick one: 1,4,2 whiffs vs SSR/SWR from 1st block, b+3,2 vs SSR from 1st block, 4,3,f+4 vs SSR from 2nd block, 4,3,4* (held) vs SWR from 2nd block. No Wavu note picks the left. The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written) agrees: "SSR in neutral, SSR after jab (you can SSR d2 and d4 now)", and calls him "weak tracking". The @Mezerdie chart (via sidestep.md) also says SSR.

Exceptions (anti-strat page, untested):
• When you are -4 — e.g. after blocking b+1 (Wavu: +4 on block): "SSL at -4 (SSL avoids df2,1 along with all the other stuff after b1)". Step left there, not right.
• Tracks your SSL: "b4, 3+4, JU, m4 will hit SSL" — b+4, 3+4, f,n,b+2 (Jet Upper) and f,F+4 (Mach Kick, also Wavu-homing). So the SSL-at-minus-4 step loses to these.
• SWA.3 (qcb+3, his Sway low): "QCB3 still have to be SWL, but you can SSR jabs after."
• Four moves and both generic throws are homing (list below).

So the habit is: SSR in neutral and after his jab; SSL only when he's +4 on you (after b+1); don't step 1+2, df+4, f,F+4 or df+3.

Standing Block Punishment

Three traps. (1) Extensions: lots of his punishable strings keep going — f+2,1 (-10) into f+2,1,4 (-13), uf+2,2,2 (-10) into ,3 (-15), 4,3 (-10) into 4,3,4 (-12) or the held 4,3,4* (-6). Confirm the string ended, or punish the worst ending. (2) Stance cancels: d+1+2 (-10) can go to SLS (-3) or SWA (-5); b+2,1 / 1,4,2,1 (±0) go to SLS at +7; df+2,1 goes to Sway at -1. (3) Pushback: the anti-strat flags 3+4, 3,3,4, b+4 and DF+1,…,2 — use a long-range punisher. f+2,1,SNE.2,3,2: "Last hit -10, but pushback, only punish at wall" (Wavu: -13).

Low Block Punishment

Reactability tag (Mike's reaction rule, using the low's fastest startup frame): Reactable 25f or slower · Mostly reactable 21–24f · Not reactable — guess or condition 20f or faster. Startups are from the Wavu movelist (for a string's low hit, Wavu's startup for that hit); a low with no stated startup gets no tag.
Bryan's quick lows — d+3, d+4, db+3, d+3+4 (i15–19) — are only -11/-12 and unreactable, so block them on a read and take the small punish (db+3 recovers crouching). The big ones are slow: df+3 (i29, -26, homing, high-crushes) and b,df+4 (i29, -13). His string lows (1,2,3, 3,2,1,4, b+3,2,1,4) are i22–24 — watch for them. Anti-strat: "CH / timing character" — most of his damage comes from counter hits, so blocking low and taking the small punish is the right trade.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus stance entries?

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. Wavu says Bryan can cancel SLS to block on frames 1~9 from a manual qcf only — not from the string transitions below. No source says he can block in Sway.
State changes the read: a standing jab is a high. Wavu lists SLS as crouching 1~25 and Sway as crouching 1~10 for the manual entries; it doesn't say whether the string transitions inherit that. If they do, a jab can whiff no matter what the arithmetic says — check with a mid. No SLS or Sway move is a power crush, crush or tracking move per Wavu.

Lab drill — training wheels: (1) Stage 1 — dummy throws a standalone 1 (jab); block it, then SSR right away, 20-rep sets, until 18/20 on three straight sets (direction only, timing untested). (2) Dummy does a standalone b+1 (+4 on block); block it, then SSL, same sets. Stage 2 — the real 1,4,2 (SSR from 1st block) with randomized delay (Wavu: it can be delayed 16f), one branch at a time. Because 1,2 and 1,4 jail, the only decision is right after the 1st block — which is exactly what Stage 1 trains. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
