## The repeat patterns

### 1. The first Reina of a session beats you: 1–6 in those game ones
Evidence: the six "yes" rows above that open with a loss (26 Sep, 27 Sep, 12 Oct, 23 Oct 8:08, 1 Nov 7:50, 1 Nov 7:43). The only first-of-session game one you won was 8 Nov. When it's your second or third Reina of the night, game one goes 2–1.

What it points to: you're playing game one on your general Victor habits and working her out live. By the second game, or the second Reina, you've remembered the matchup. This is the "why game one goes wrong" answer the data supports. Which specific habit loses that game is **theory**, because no ranked Reina loss is on video (see pattern 5).

Guide match: the Practice priorities section already says "she's beating you before you've adjusted", and the Weekly Lab Plan's 5-minute ranked warm-up and Game-one rules vs Reina were built for exactly this. They started 17 Nov, after your last ranked Reina game, so they haven't been tested in a real set yet.

Missing from the guide: nothing names the trigger. The useful rule is "the first time Reina shows up in a session, play the game-one rules", not "every Reina game". A 30-second pre-set refresher, read on the character-select screen, would cover it.

### 2. It isn't skill: you drop game one even when you're the stronger player
Evidence: 12 Oct (you +139) and 23 Oct 8:49 (you +107) were both game-one losses at 2–3, followed by two straight wins. Three of the seven game-one losses went 2–3, so most were close, not blowouts. Across the seven you won 8 rounds and lost 21.

What it points to: matchup knowledge, not fundamentals. A higher-rated you losing a close one, then sweeping, is a player missing information in game one and finding it by game two.

Guide match: the guide has the knowledge (punish buckets, SEN, Heat). The gap is getting it into game one, which is pattern 1's fix.

### 3. When she's your level or better and takes game one, you don't get a game back
Evidence: the three sets where you took zero games were 26 Sep (+7), 23 Oct 8:08 (+7) and 1 Nov 7:43 (+114). Against a Reina rated at or above you, losing game one meant a game back only once in four sets (27 Sep, +61). Against lower-rated Reinas you won a game back all three times.

What it points to: against an even or stronger Reina, losing game one costs the set, so game one matters most exactly when you're most likely to lose it.

Guide match: no section covers this. It's another reason pattern 1's fix is the priority.

### 4. She adjusts back after you win
Evidence: 1 Nov morning, both sets. 7:50 AM: L, W 3–0, then L 2–3. 8:19 AM: W 3–2, then L 1–3. Your coach note for that session says the same thing ("she adjusted after losing"). On 23 Oct, by contrast, two of your three Reina sets ended with you winning back-to-back.

What it points to: after a win, keep changing, don't repeat the plan that just worked. Small sample (two sets), so treat it as **theory**.
