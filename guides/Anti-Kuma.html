Kuma on P1 side — inputs face right

Movement — Which Way to Step

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written) opens with one word: "SSR". The @Mezerdie sidestep chart (via sidestep.md) also says SSR. Wavu's own per-move notes are thin for Kuma — most of them say either side — and the one clear SSR call is his wake-up. No source contradicts SSR.

Exceptions:
• Charge moves (db+1,2, ws1,2, HBS.1,2 held): anti-strat says "Full charge extremely linear, SS both ways. Medium charge: SSL seems the most consistent. Smaller charge might require SWL. Tiny delay will hit everything." So against a held charge, step LEFT, not right. The anti-strat page also says punishing the first hit (db+1 -10, ws1 -11, HBS.1 -13) beats medium and max hold.
• SS.1+2: Wavu counterplay — "SSL after SS 1+2, or sidewalk the charged second hit."
• b,f+2 (i15 high, CH launcher): Wavu — "Tracks very well. Duck it when possible." Don't step it.
• Four moves are homing (list below), and both generic throws home too.

So the habit is: SSR in neutral and on his wake-up; SSL when you see him hold a charge; duck b,f+2.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus stance entries?

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range, the fastest frame is used (and the lower advantage of an advantage range). Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered.

Lab drill — training wheels: (1) Stage 1 — dummy throws a standalone db+1; block it, then SSL right away, 20-rep sets, until 18/20 on three straight sets (direction only, timing untested). (2) Dummy lies face-up and does FUFT.d+1+2; SSR it, same sets. Stage 2 — the real db+1,2* (medium charge) with randomized hold length, one charge level at a time. Stage 3 — mix db+1 alone (-10, punish), db+1,2 uncharged (-13, block and punish), medium charge (SSL) and full charge (+3, either side) — and try the anti-strat's answer, punishing the first hit, against all of them. Kuma's charge strings put the read on hold length, so Stage 3 is where the real decision lives. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
