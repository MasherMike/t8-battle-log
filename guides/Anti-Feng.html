Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep / sidewalk LEFT (SSL, SWL) in neutral. Set by Tier 2. The TekkenDocs anti-strat page (community-written) calls Feng an "oppressive poking character with strong evasion". It splits his neutral moves by direction rather than naming one side, but its one whole-situation call is "SSL or SWL strong" after his b+3. Wavu's Feng counterplay page lists its general options as "SWL · SSL duck · SSL back dash · SSL block", and the TekkenDocs sidestep chart (community chart by @Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) also says SSL.

It's a close call, and the exceptions matter more than the default. At 0 frames the anti-strat page lists 3 moves to SSL (d+2, uf+3, df+3) and 4 to SSR (uf+4, df+4, db+1, f+1+2). df+4 and db+1 are everyday pokes, so the raw tally leans slightly right.

Exceptions:
• f+4 and f+1+2 track your SSL. Wavu: "Good left sidestep tracking" on both. These two are the moves the default loses to. Both are highs, so ducking beats them too (see Duck and Launch).
• String extensions that whiff to the right (Tier 1, Wavu): f+4,2 from 1st block, df+4,2,1+2 and b+2,3,4 from 2nd block, 1,1 vs SWR from 1st block. Tier 1 beats the default for these moves.
• Left still works on: 3,1+2 (whiffs vs SSL, SWL) and, inconsistently, b+4,H.3+4 (vs SSL from 1st block).
• Homing — don't step: df+3+4, db+4, SS.2, STC.3 and both generic throws.

So the habit is: SWL/SSL his neutral, duck or SSR when you expect f+4 or f+1+2, and step right only after blocking the strings named above.

Evidence tier: the default comes from Tier 2: the TekkenDocs anti-strat page plus Wavu's counterplay prose, with the sidestep chart agreeing. Tier 1 (per-move Wavu/TekkenDocs notes) has 11 step notes and the two "left sidestep tracking" tags. The tiers disagree on four string extensions and on f+4/f+1+2, where Tier 1 says right. The guide follows Tier 1 for those moves. The anti-strat page also says that after b+3, "b1/db1/jab are homing, b4 and d4 'always homing'". Theory (Ledger): Ducking covers both of the moves that track SSL. f+4 and f+1+2 are highs, so a crouch makes them whiff whichever way you were stepping. A duck read is safer than SSR here, because SSR loses to the moves the anti-strat page says to SSL (d+2, uf+3, df+3). But a duck loses to his mids (df+4, db+1).

Homing — don't step these at all

df+3+4 (mid, −7) · db+4 (low, i31, −26, tornado on hit: block low and launch) · SS.2 (high, +3, goes BT: duck it) · STC.3 (high from Shifting Clouds, +6, goes BT: duck it) · 1+3 and 2+4 (throws). All Wavu.

Low Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Reactability tag (Mike's reaction rule, using the low's fastest startup frame): Reactable 25f or slower · Mostly reactable 21–24f · Not reactable — guess or condition 20f or faster. Startups are from the Wavu movelist; a low with no stated startup gets no tag. For a string, the tag is for the low hit named on the row, using Wavu's startup for it. Stance lows are tagged on their raw startup; out of a plus stance entry they arrive sooner (see Stance Check).
Feng has a lot of lows and almost all are punishable. Five are slow enough to react to: db+4 (i31, −26), f+4,4 (i33, −31), db+2,2 (i28, −17), db+1+2,2 (i29) and 3,3,4 (i25). The rest you block on a guess. Many recover crouching (FC or c): punish with a WS launcher. TekkenDocs gives high-crush frames on most of them (d+2 6~51, d+3 4~52, d+3+4 4~46, db+2 6~30, db+3 6~51, db+4 6~58), so a high jab whiffs into them.

Shifting Clouds (STC)

Entries: f+3+4 parry (3~18) on absorbing one hit · f,F+3 / BT.3 ~f at +8c on block or hit · Heat Smash H.2+3 on block (+12) · db+1~F (−16 on block, +0 on hit). The f+3+4 parry catches "high and mid fist and feet moves (not elbows/knees/heads etc)" (Wavu).

Heat↑ Return to Top

Practice priorities

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a startup range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered. Airborne (a), knockdown (d) and ground (g) hit advantages are left out, so f+4~B (+56a) and 3~4~B (+24a) aren't tabled.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. Wavu says otherwise for Kenpo ("i20 high/mid block"); no source says Feng can block in STC, CD or BT.
State changes the read (Wavu/TekkenDocs): at +N your jab lands on his frame 10+N. STC.1 is a power crush 8~19, so it absorbs the jab at +8 (frame 18) and is past its armor at +12 (frame 22). A standing jab is a high, so it whiffs against the crouching lows (CD.1 6~19, db+3 6~51, BT.d+3 6~52, BT.d+1 and KNP.1 from 15) and against any crouching state. KNP.3 is airborne 15~30 (beats lows, not a high jab).
