Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep LEFT (SSL) up close; sidewalk RIGHT (SWR) at range. The TekkenDocs anti-strat page (community-written) opens with "SSL block close, SWR at range (WR3, ff4), SWL for ff3." The TekkenDocs sidestep chart (community chart by @Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) also says SSL. Wavu's per-move notes are close to even: outside Heat, three step notes favour the left side and two the right.

Exceptions:
• d+3,4 (low, then high) and BT.1+2,4 (mid, mid, high, +3, Heat Engager): Wavu — "Whiffs vs SSR … from 1st block." These go right. Both enders are highs, so a duck works too (see Duck and Launch).
• In Heat: H.f+2,1,2 and H.f,F+1,2 whiff vs SSR / SWR from the 1st block (Wavu). The anti-strat page agrees ("SSR last hit") and warns that f,F+1,3/4 and f+2,1,4 will still hit you.
• df+1 (i14 mid, high-crushes 6~13): anti-strat — "good tracking. Have to SWL most of the time."
• Homing — don't step: f+4 (high, +1), b+2 (mid, into BT), df+3 (low, −23), HYP.3 (low, from Hypnotist), the BT throws BT.f+1+3 / BT.f+2+3, and both generic throws. b+1+2 has "pseudo homing (strong realign properties)".
• Stances (anti-strat): against BT, "SWL (at −3) beats everything except BT2, BT4, caliroll". Against HYP, "An SSL duck covers all options from a −2 transition". Against AOP, stepping "seems risky (even on 0) because AoP2 'homing'". Wavu lists no homing tag on AOP.2; that word is the anti-strat page's.

So the habit is: SSL her close-range pressure, SWR when she's running in from range, and step right only for the d+3,4 and BT.1+2,4 enders (or duck them). Don't step anything homing.

Evidence tier: the SSL default is set by Tier 2, the TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written), which the sidestep chart by @Mezerdie (via sidestep.md) matches. Tier 1 (Wavu per-move notes) has seven step notes. Outside Heat they lean left 3–2: SSL/SWL beats 1,d+2,1+2, hFC.3,2 and f+2,1,4 (SWL only), and SSR beats d+3,4 and BT.1+2,4. With the two Heat-only notes (both SSR) the raw count is 4–3 the other way. So Tier 1 doesn't settle a default on its own, and the tiers don't conflict: where a Tier 1 note names a move, the table follows it. The range rows (SWR vs f,f,F+3 and f,F+4, SWL vs f,F+3 and df+1) and the stance rows are anti-strat claims with no Wavu step note behind them. Nothing in this section is lab-tested.

Standing Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Block values are from Wavu's Xiaoyu movelist and counterplay tables. 171 moves are on both sites and TekkenDocs agrees on every block value. Where Wavu gives a range (e.g. −14~−8), the move goes in the tier of the less minus number.

Wavu counterplay: "when trying to punish her, try to use a mid with a relatively low hitbox." Many of her moves duck or jump, and a high punish can whiff.

Three traps. (1) BT cancels: u+1+2,2,1 and f,F+2,1 are −12, but both "Cancel to BT r38 with B (−19/−8) at frame 25". The cancel is worse for her (−19): punish harder if she cancels. AOP.2,1 cancels to BT at −14. (2) Power crush after absorbing: ub+4 and BT.b+1+2 are −13 / −14 normally but only −9 after absorbing your hit (Wavu). (3) Her i8 mid: b+1 is −12 on block, but it comes out at i8~13 (anti-strat: "This is very often safe, be careful"). On hit it goes to BT.

Duck and Launch↑ Return to Top

Her plus highs are the duck targets. f,F+4 is +9 into Hypnotist on block and HYP.3+4 is +8, but both are highs. The 4 of BT.1+2,4 (+3, Heat Engager) is a high too. Ducking takes the plus frames away. Her power crushes (ub+4, BT.b+1+2) are mids: don't duck them. Her BT and AOP throws are slow (i31~33): duck those on reaction.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. For Xiaoyu Wavu says it outright: AOP "Cannot block in stance", HYP "Cannot block during stance". No source says she can block in BT. Her three stances feed into each other: many BT moves go to AOP or HYP and back.

HYP — Hypnotist

Entered: db+1+2 (right) / ub+1+2 (left), actionable for 70F, and with B after f+2,1, 1,d+2, hFC.3,2,1, BT.2,1 and AOP.1. Plus on block: f,F+4 (+9) and the BT Heat Smash H.BT.2+3 (+9, at the 2nd step).

BT — Backturn

General Counter Play↑ Return to Top

Practice priorities

1. Check her with mids, not jabs. Wavu: "it is vitally important to check her with mids first and foremost." df+1 high-crushes 6~13, every AOP move high-crushes, BT.1 has jab evasion, and HYP and BT both parry punches.
2. Duck her plus highs: f,F+4 (+9 into HYP), HYP.3+4 (+8), the 4 of BT.1+2,4 (+3), b+3 (+3 BT), f+4 (+1, homing), and the i31 BT / AOP throws.
3. Launch the big ones: AOP.3+4 (−33), BT.d+4 (−26), df+3 (−23, reactable), f+3,1,4 (−22), BT.4 / BT.2,1,4 / uf,n,4 / hFC.3,2,1,4 (−18), AOP.uf+3 (−17), f+1+2 and BT.3+4 (−16).
4. Don't press into HYP or BT at +9. After f,F+4 or either Heat Smash, HYP.4 / BT.2 beat your jab, and both stances parry punches.
5. SSL up close, SWR at range, SSR (or duck) the d+3,4 and BT.1+2,4 enders.

Lab drill — training wheels: have the dummy throw the standalone opener, then step. Stage 1 (direction only, timing untested): the dummy plays 1,d+2 and stops, on fixed timing, and you sidestep left after the 2nd block (Wavu: 1,d+2,1+2 whiffs vs SSL / SWL from 2nd block). 20-rep sets, graduate at 18/20 on 3 straight sets (about 60 reps minimum). Stage 2: the dummy plays the real 1,d+2,1+2 with randomized delay (1,d+2 can be delayed 10F; Wavu says interrupt with i11 from 2nd block, so there is room to step), 16/20 on 3 straight sets. Stage 3: random playback of 1,d+2,1+2 (SSL), 1,d+2~B into HYP (−2: anti-strat SSL then duck), 1,d+2 into BT (−3: anti-strat SWL), d+3,4 (the one that goes right, or stay crouched) and a throw, 25-rep sets, 75% correct decisions on 2 straight sets. Stage 4: log what holds in real matches as Lab (Mike). Why Stage 3 matters for her: 1,d+2 can end in a mid, or branch to HYP or BT, and each wants a different answer, so the step has to become a read, not a reflex. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
