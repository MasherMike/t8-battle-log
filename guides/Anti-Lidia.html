Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR) in neutral. The TekkenDocs sidestep chart (community chart by @Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) says SSR for Lidia. TekkenDocs has no anti-strat page for her yet. Wavu's per-move notes agree on balance: two step notes say SSR, one says SSL.

Exceptions:
• 1,2,3 (all highs): Wavu — "Whiffs vs SSL, SWL from 1st block." This one goes left. It's also all high, so a duck after 1,2 beats it too (see Duck and Launch).
• Homing — don't step: df+3+4, db+3 (low), b+4 (high), qcf+4 (high, +5 into HRS), and from Horse Stance HRS.1 (high, power crush), HRS.3 (mid) and HRS.3+4 (high). Both generic throws are homing too.
• Horse Stance (HRS): Wavu counterplay — "You generally don't want to SSR and duck while she is in this stance." Three of its six attacks are homing.
• Heaven and Earth (HAE) at +5: the same Wavu page says the opposite for HAE: "There is an option select: SSR and duck or throw, since she has an automatic parry in this stance." At +6 or more into HAE: "There is no counterplay: you have to take the 50/50."

So the habit is: SSR her neutral strings (3,2 and f+3,2 both whiff it), step left only for 1,2,3, and don't step anything homing or anything from Horse Stance.

Standing Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Block values are from Wavu's Lidia movelist and counterplay tables. 167 moves are on both sites and TekkenDocs agrees on every block value. Where Wavu gives a range (e.g. −11~−10), the move goes in the tier of the less minus number.

Wavu counterplay on her design: "Very strong neutral buttons with lots of plus frames." Most of her pokes (df+1 −5, df+4 −4, f+4 −4, ws1 −3, ws4 −4) are safe; the punishes are mostly string enders.

Three traps. (1) Naked vs extension: f+3 is −11 but f+3,2 is −4 (into WLF) and f+3,4 is +5 into HRS. Wavu: interrupt with i5 / i8 from the 1st block, so the extensions arrive before your punish. Punish f+3 only when it stopped. qcf+1+2 is −12 but qcf+1+2,2 is −4 (into WLF) and the charged qcf+1+2,2* is +8. f,F+4 is −13 but f,F+4,3 is −27: wait for the 2nd hit. (2) Power crush after absorbing: b+1+2 is −13 but −8 if it absorbed your hit; HAE.1 is −11 but −7 after absorbing. Wavu states both. (3) Heat Engagers: punishing HRS.2 (−10), FC.df+2 (−14) or f+1+2,3,1+2 (−15) also stops a Heat activation.

Duck and Launch↑ Return to Top

Her high enders are where the duck pays. f+3,4 is +5 into HRS on block, but the 4 is a high. Duck it instead of blocking it and the plus frames are gone. The same goes for b+3,4, df+1,2 and df+4,2. Her power crush (b+1+2) is a mid, but her Horse Stance power crush (HRS.1) is a high: duck that one.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. No source says Lidia can block in HRS, CAT, WLF or HAE. Each stance has a cancel back to standing (HRS.b, CAT.b, WLF.b, HAE.b), and Wavu says she's "limited to blocking" for 7 frames after it (13 frames from HRS, which "takes 8f to cancel into block"). Each stance also has a back-dash (HRS.b,B etc., airborne 11~25). In Heat, H.HRS.U / H.CAT.U / H.WLF.U parry all highs and mids on frames 5~39 and go to HAE.

General Counter Play↑ Return to Top

Practice priorities

1. Don't jab after her +5 HRS entries (f,F+2, f+3,4, qcf+4). HRS.1 (power crush) absorbs the jab and HRS.1+2 crouches under it. Block, and only crouch on a read for HRS.1+2 (−18).
2. Duck her plus highs: the 4 of f+3,4 (+5), qcf+4 (+5, homing), df+4~3 (+8), HRS.3+4 (+6), f,f,F+1 (+6).
3. Launch the big ones: f+1+2,3,1+2 and b+3,4,1+2 (−15), qcf+2,2,1+2 (−17), f,F+4,3 (−27), and the lows FC.df+3 (−26), HRS.1+2 and CAT.4,3+4 (−18), d+3 (−16, but check for d+3,1).

5. Deny Heat: punish HRS.2 (−10) and FC.df+2 (−14), duck CAT.3.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt her plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = her raw startup − her frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a startup range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered. Where Wavu gives a separate "<stance> transition is X" number, that number is used instead of the move's block value (so 1,2,2, 2,2, uf+4, f+3,2, qcf+1+2,2, CAT.4 and db+3 are minus entries and left out). Airborne (a) and knockdown (d) hit advantages are left out.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. No source says Lidia can block in HRS, CAT, WLF or HAE.
State changes the read (Wavu/TekkenDocs): at +N your jab lands on her frame 10+N. A power crush absorbs it inside its window: HRS.1 (7~16) and HAE.1 (7~17) absorb the jab at every on-block entry below (+5 HRS; +1, +5, +6 HAE); at larger advantages they already land first. A standing jab is a high, so it whiffs against crouching moves (HRS.1+2 from 8, CAT.4 11~31). WLF.1 has jab evasion. HRS.3 is airborne 9~27, which beats lows, not a high jab. Homing: HRS.1, HRS.3, HRS.3+4.

Lab drill — training wheels: have the dummy throw the standalone opener, then step. Stage 1 (direction only, timing untested): the dummy plays a lone 3 on fixed timing, and you sidestep right after blocking it (Wavu: 3,2 whiffs vs SSR from 1st block). 20-rep sets, graduate at 18/20 on 3 straight sets (about 60 reps minimum). Stage 2: the dummy plays the real 3,2 with randomized timing (it's a combo from 1st hit with only a 3f delay, and Wavu says interrupt with i5, so the step has to start right after the 1st block), 16/20 on 3 straight sets. Stage 3: random playback of 3,2, f+3,2 (also SSR), f+3,4 (duck), 1,2,3 (step left) and a throw, 25-rep sets, 75% correct decisions on 2 straight sets. Stage 4: log what holds in real matches as Lab (Mike). Why Stage 3 matters for her: her f+3 opener branches into a mid (step right) or a +5 high (duck), and 1,2,3 goes the other way, so the step has to become a read, not a reflex. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
