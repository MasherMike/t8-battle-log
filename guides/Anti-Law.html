Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). Set by Tier 2, the TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written): "SSR pretty strong against him. Watch out for df2 clipping." The TekkenDocs sidestep chart (@Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) also says SSR, so the two community sources agree. No Tier 1 data: neither Wavu nor TekkenDocs has a single per-move whiff or sidestep note for Law; the only tracking facts in the movelists are the homing flags below.

Exceptions (all from the anti-strat page):
• df+2 (mid, i15~16, −7, +34a launch on normal hit) can clip your SSR. Of all the moves to get caught stepping, this is the expensive one.
• b+2 (mid, i16, −8): SSL is "more consistent".
• db+3 (low, i17, −13): SWL.
• Dragon Sign Stance (DSS): SSR is strong against the stance in general, but SSL beats "Nunchuck / DSS f2" and SWL beats DSS.4. The page then adds that after ss3 on hit (+7, you're slightly off-axis) "Now SSR > DSS4", so the DSS.4 answer depends on how he entered.
• Homing (Wavu), don't step: db+4 (low, i26), b+3, b+4, ub+1+2, BT.1+2, DSS.3 and his 1+3 / 2+4 throws.

So the habit is: SSR his neutral pokes and the stance; switch to SSL against b+2; block df+2 when he's plus instead of stepping into it.

Theory (Ledger): The anti-strat page gives two answers to DSS.4: SWL (tested "from +5") and SSR after ss3 on hit. DSS.4 is a low at i21 and −13 on block (Wavu), so by Mike's reaction rule it is mostly reactable, and blocking it gets a 13f punish. Stand-blocking in DSS and crouching on reaction to the low may be more reliable than picking a step direction by entry. Boundary: it only works when he enters DSS at +5 or less; at +7 and up (4,3 / 4,3,4 / ws3 / Heat Smash) the low arrives earlier. Test: dummy enters DSS with ws4 (+4 on block) and plays DSS.4 or DSS.f+1 at random; react-crouch the low and count clean blocks.
