Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: lean Sidestep RIGHT (SSR), but she's a weak tracker either way. The TekkenDocs anti-strat page opens with "Reina is hard to track" and lists four of her pokes that lose to SSR (df+2, db+4, df+4, 1,2) against one that loses to SSL (df+1). The @Mezerdie sidestep chart says SSL instead, so the two sources disagree; this guide follows the anti-strat page because it names moves, while the chart gives one whole-character direction. Treat SSR as a lean, not a rule.

Exceptions — these are the ones that catch the SSR habit:
• df+1 (i13~14, −3, goes into SEN) is the one listed poke that loses to SSL, and it's one of her most-used buttons.
• 3, b+2 and SEN.3 are homing — stepping does nothing; see the list below. 3 and SEN.3 are highs, so duck them.
• Wavu notes that 2~1 "tracks to the left."
• From WRA (Heaven's Wrath) stepping is limited: the anti-strat page says backdash her lows and throws there instead.

If you only drill three things:

Parries — what goes through

Three parries, none of them stop lows or throws. b+1+3 Tatenashi parries mids and highs (frames 3~9; the anti-strat page compares it to Jin's parry, and says her follow-ups and quick moves after it generally hit through armor). d+3 Unsoku Kou is a punch parry (frames 5~12) that goes to WGS. Heat H.db+1+2 auto-parries mids and highs for frames 20~60. If she's fishing, use lows, throws and, against d+3, kicks.

Lab drill — training wheels: have the dummy throw the standalone poke, then step. Stage 1 (direction only, timing untested): dummy alternates df+2 and df+1 on fixed timing; SSR the df+2, SSL the df+1, 20-rep sets, graduate at 18/20 on 3 straight sets. Stage 2: random playback of the two, 16/20 on 3 straight sets — now you're reading the startup, not remembering the order. Stage 3: add db+4 (SSR), 3 (homing high, duck it) and df+1~SEN into SEN.3 (duck it), 25-rep sets, 75% correct decisions on 2 straight sets. Stage 4: log what holds in real matches as Lab (Mike). Why Stage 3 matters for her: her homing highs punish a step reflex, and SEN.3 is homing, so the read has to come back in. Weaknesses: a standalone poke has no follow-up on a clock, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Cap a session around 100–150 reps; stop if accuracy drops 15 points from where you started; go back a stage if you're under 60% for two sets. Rep counts are a rule of thumb, not research.
