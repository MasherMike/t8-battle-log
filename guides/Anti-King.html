Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written): "Grappler. Generally SSR. SSL: db3(or SWR), df3." Wavu counterplay agrees: "Against King, however, sidestep right and duck (SSR + duck) is often a useful defensive approach." The @Mezerdie chart (via sidestep.md) also says SSR.

Exceptions:
• df+3 (i14 mid, -9): Wavu — "Has good right sidestep tracking." It catches SSR. Block it, or step left (anti-strat). This is the move that stopped Mike's SSR.
• db+3 (i23 low, -14): the sources disagree. The anti-strat says SSL (or SWR) beats it; Wavu and TekkenDocs notes say it "Tracks left side". Per-move notes (Tier 1) outrank the overview, so don't SSL it — the anti-strat's other answer, SWR, is the one that fits both. Better still: it's an i23 low at -14, so block it and punish.
• Steps both ways (anti-strat): f,F+2, f,f,n,2, uf+4, db+2, f+3 (SWL at -3), b+1, f+2, uf+3+4.
• 1,2,1: anti-strat — "SSR! The next hit of the 10-string will also whiff. The throw will track, but just break it with 2."
• Homing — don't step: f+4, uf+1, f,F+1, ws2, JGR.3, the RKO (JGR.1+3), ss3+4 ("Tracks side steps"), and every generic throw. During Heat his command throws become homing too (Wavu: "Homing during heat").

So the habit is: SSR by default; block df+3 instead of stepping it; never SSL db+3; don't step the homing list. Wavu counterplay adds a warning: "Be careful not to duck recklessly, as King has strong mids to punish predictable defense."

Standing Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Three traps. (1) Naked vs extension: f+2 is -11, but f+2,1 is -9 (Heat Engager) and f+2,d+1 -13c (crouching — WS punish); 1+2 is -11 (King ends backturned) while 1+2,3 is -3; b+1 is -8 but b+1,2 is -13; ws2 -9 but ws2,2 -12. (2) Jaguar Sprint transitions: 3,2,2 is -13, but the held 3,2,2* is +9c into JGR on block — the anti-strat flags 3,2,2 for the wall. 1+2,1 can cancel with B to -18 (anti-strat: "-16 on cancel"). (3) Power crush on block: b+3 and uf+3+4 become -5 / -7 if they absorbed a hit first (Wavu).

Low Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Reactability tag (Mike's reaction rule, using the low's fastest startup frame): Reactable 25f or slower · Mostly reactable 21–24f · Not reactable — guess or condition 20f or faster. Startups are from the Wavu movelist (for a string's low hit, Wavu's startup for that hit); a low with no stated startup gets no tag.
King's everyday lows are fast and punishable: d+4 (i14, -15), df+4 (i14~15, -14), d+3 (i17, -12), db+3 (i23, -14, high crush 6~), and the Ali Kicks d+3+4 (i16, -25). Wavu counterplay's "Lows" bucket: f,f,n,2, db+3, d+3+4,4,4, d+3+4,4,4,2. The ones you can see coming are FC.df+1 (i32~34), db+4 (i29~31) and the 3rd hit of df+4,3,d+4. His d+1 / d+2 jabs are special lows (sl) — small minus, no punish. Several recover crouching (FC) — use WS punishes.

Duck and Launch↑ Return to Top

Wavu counterplay: "Duck selectively: Look for predictable highs and lows in his strings, but avoid ducking without a read." His crouch throws (d+1+3, d+2+4, d+1+4) are unbreakable and only grab crouching opponents — they are what makes ducking a guess. Anti-strat "Duckable highs": 1+2,3, b+1,2, b+2,1, b+2,1+2, BT.1+2 (on reaction), df+2,1, f+2,1, df+4,3.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

King's stances: Jaguar Sprint (JGR), Jaguar Step (JGS) and backturn (BT). Wavu counterplay: "Respect Jaguar Sprint during Heat: Its enhanced options make King's stance pressure particularly dangerous."

Throws↑ Return to Top

Wavu counterplay: "A throw that looks like a 1+2 break may actually require a 1 break… Standing throws: usually look for a 1 break. Running throws: usually require a 1+2 break, especially Shining Wizard (f,f,F+2+4). On counter hit, King's throws become extremely difficult to break." Anti-strat: "GS looks like 1+2 break but is a 1 break." Generic 1+3 / 2+4 are homing, break 1 or 2 (Wavu). Left side throw: break 1. Right side throw: break 2. Back throws are unbreakable. Crouch throws (d+1+3, d+2+4, d+1+4) are unbreakable. Chain throws: anti-strat — "Chain throws with kicharge broken with 1."

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered: JGR and BT. Jaguar Step (JGS) has no entry with a stated advantage (it starts from neutral 3+4), so it isn't checked.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. No source says King blocks in JGR or BT (Wavu: from the manual sprint he can cancel to a standing block with b from frame 35).
Properties that change the read (Wavu): in Heat the sprint is a power crush (f+3+4 pc7~89; db+1+2,3+4 pc7~) — no source states armor on the 3,2,2 / H.2+3 / ws2,2 / uf+3+4 transitions, but the anti-strat says "Jaguar sprint has armor" in Heat, so treat any Heat JGR as armored until tested. JGR.3 is homing and airborne 11~30; JGR.4 airborne 7~23; JGR.1+3 (RKO) is a homing unbreakable throw; BT.1+4 is airborne 8~; BT.1+2 / BT.1+4 are unblockable. Wavu notes no power crush on any JGR or BT attack.

Lab drill — training wheels: Stage 1 — dummy throws a standalone b+1 (i12 high) and f+2 (i15~16 mid); block, then SSR right away, 20-rep sets, until 18/20 on three straight sets (direction only, timing untested). Separate set: standalone df+3 — the answer is block, don't step (or SSL). Stage 2 — the real 1,2,1 (SSR after 1,2), b+1,2 and f+2,1 with randomized delay, one branch at a time. Stage 3 — mix df+3, f+2,1 vs f+2,d+1, a Tomahawk / Giant Swing / Tijuana Twister and a crouch throw, so the step-duck-break decision is live. King's strings branch into throws and crouch throws, so the post-block decision is step, stand-and-break, or duck — Stage 1 trains only the direction. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
