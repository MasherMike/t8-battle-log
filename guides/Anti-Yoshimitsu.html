Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). Set by Tier 2, the TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written), which opens with one word: "SSR". The two community sources disagree: the TekkenDocs sidestep chart (@Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) says SSL for Yoshimitsu. The guides' evidence order ranks the anti-strat Quick overview above the chart, so the guide follows SSR and keeps the chart's SSL visible here. The Tier 1 notes don't settle it: one Wavu note backs SSR (2,3), one punishes it (uf+1), and Wavu's counterplay page gives opposite sides for his two unblockables.

Exceptions:
• uf+1 (mid, i17, −10): Wavu, "Good right sidestep tracking". It catches the default step. Block it and punish −10.
• The two unblockables have opposite weak sides (Wavu counterplay: "Sword right, spit left"): FC.DF+1 Sword Sweep (unblockable low) → SWR or interrupt; DB+1+2 Bad Stomach → BDS.1 Poison Breath (unblockable high) → SWL or duck.
• ub+1+3 Sword Stab (unblockable mid, i50): the anti-strat says SSR "seems inconsistent": walk or SSL.
• Dragonfly (DGF): SSR beats DGF.4 and DGF.1, SSL beats DGF.3 (anti-strat). DGF.f+2 and DGF.f+1+2 are homing (Wavu).
• Homing (Wavu), don't step: f+3, uf+4, KIN.4, KIN.f+2, hFC.df+4 / IND.4, IND.3+4, the DGF/FLE slashes, and both standard throws.

So the habit is: SSR his neutral pokes; block uf+1; walk right against the sword, left against the spit.

Evidence tier: the default comes from Tier 2 (TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview: "SSR"). Tier 2 sources disagree: the sidestep chart says SSL; the Quick overview is the higher-priority Tier 2 source, so SSR stands. Tier 1 (Wavu per-move notes) gives two rows: 2,3 whiffs SSR (agrees) and uf+1 tracks SSR (an exception, not a different default). Wavu also says d+3 "most commonly impacts on frame 16, except when tracking right"; it doesn't say which side, so it is not a row. Tally of named moves: right 4 (2,3, FC.DF+1, DGF.4, DGF.1), left 3 (BDS.1, ub+1+3, DGF.3), one tracks right (uf+1). It's close. SSR is a neutral default, not a rule. Nothing in this section is lab-tested.
Theory (Ledger): In Dragonfly, crouching may cover more than stepping. Per Wavu, DGF.2, DGF.2,4 (1st hit), DGF.f+2, DGF.f+1+2 and the unblockable attack-throw DGF.3+4 are all high (three of them homing, so a step loses to them), and DGF.3 is a low a crouch blocks. A crouch loses to only two moves: DGF.4 (mid, i18~20, −4, +14g on hit) and DGF.1 (mid, i30~31, −2c). The anti-strat says SSR beats both of those, so the read is crouch by default and SSR when you expect the mid. Check: DGF is airborne from frame 1 on every DGF attack (Wavu js1~), which matters for your counter-attacks (lows and throws whiff) but not for ducking his highs. Test: dummy enters DGF with 3~4 and plays all DGF moves at random; hold crouch and count what hits you.

Reactability tag (Mike's reaction rule, using the low's fastest startup frame): Reactable 25f or slower · Mostly reactable 21–24f · Not reactable — guess or condition 20f or faster. Startups are from the Wavu movelist; a low with no stated startup gets no tag. For a string, the tag uses the startup Wavu lists for the low hit itself (its opener when the low comes first). Stance lows are tagged on their raw startup.
His basic lows are fast and very punishable: db+3 and hFC.db+3 are −25, hFC.df+4 −26, d+3 −18~−16, d+4 −15. None of those is reactable (i12–i19). The slow ones are db+1 (i26), uf+2 (i35), KIN.3 (i25), IND.4 (i47) and the NSS crouch lows (i25). Many lows crouch early (Wavu cs: d+3 and d+4 from frame 4, db+3 from 6, db+1 from 8), so a high jab whiffs into them. Unblockable low: FC.DF+1 Sword Sweep can't be blocked at all: SWR or interrupt (see below).

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

Yoshimitsu has more stances than anyone. The ones that matter: Kincho (KIN), Manji Dragonfly (DGF), Flea (FLE), Indian Stance (IND), Meditation (MED) / back-turned (BT), Bad Stomach (BDS) and No Sword Stance (NSS), a sword mode that changes many normal moves. Can he block in a stance? No source says he can in KIN, DGF, FLE, IND, MED or BDS, so treat each as a stance that can't block. Wavu only says he cannot block during the transition into NSS (b+1+2, cs8~50).

KIN — Kincho

Entries, plus (Wavu): f,F+3~1+2 (+3 on block, +12 on hit), KIN.4~1+2 (+3 on block), uf+1~1+2 (+9 on hit; −3 on block).
Entries without a stated advantage: 2,2~1+2, ws4~1+2, b+2~1+2, b+2,1~1+2, f,n,d,df+1~1+2, df+1+4~1+2; minus: db+3,…,4~1+2 (−6). Manual: 1+2.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a startup range, the fastest startup is used.
