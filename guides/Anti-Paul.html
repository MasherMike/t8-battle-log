Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR) in neutral and at small minus frames. Set by Tier 2. The TekkenDocs anti-strat page (community-written) says: "SSR extremely strong, close at -1, avoids 80% of the movelist. Still works at -3 (f1+2 on 2P), but df1 / d1 / 3 now tracks. SSR is still strong at -5 (uf2), about same as at -3. Backdash duck is another good option." The TekkenDocs sidestep chart (community chart by @Mezerdie, via sidestep.md) also says SSR. Tier 1 agrees: of the Wavu per-move step notes, 6 whiff to the right and 2 to the left, and ub+2 has "good left sidestep tracking".

Exceptions:
• At −3 or worse, df+1, d+1 and 3 track your SSR (anti-strat). These are his fastest mids (i14, i14~15, i15~16), so block them instead of stepping.
• ub+2 tracks SSL (Wavu), so it's another reason not to step left in neutral.
• Two string branches whiff to the LEFT (Tier 1, Wavu): f,F+2,2 (SSL / SW from 1st block) and f,F+3,4,d+4 (SWL from 1st block). Both end in a low.
• Homing — don't step: f+3 (high: duck), f+4 (mid, Heat Engager), ub+2*, DPD.df+1, SWA.4 (+1, CH launcher), and his generic throws. Wavu's counterplay page adds that f+2 is "pseudo-homing on short range".

So the habit is: SSR his neutral and after his −1 to −5 moves; block when you're −3 or worse and expect df+1/d+1/3; duck the homing highs (f+3, SWA.4).

Low Block Punishment↑ Return to Top

Reactability tag (Mike's reaction rule, using the low's fastest startup frame): Reactable 25f or slower · Mostly reactable 21–24f · Not reactable — guess or condition 20f or faster. Startups are from the Wavu movelist; a low with no stated startup gets no tag. For a string, the tag is for the low hit named on the row, using Wavu's startup for it. Stance lows are tagged on their raw startup.
Demoman (d+4,2,1+2) is the matchup. d+4 is i15 and −31c on block, and every follow-up is launch-punishable too: d+4,2 −18, d+4,2,1+2 −17. Wavu: d+4 needs a Clean Hit (range 1.59) for the +14a knockdown; without it, it's 8 damage and −17 on hit. Wavu's counterplay page: "Needs a clean hit to hit you: he needs to be at kissing distance." The low to fear is the one at point-blank range.

Only three lows are slow enough to react to: f,F+2,2 (i36), f,F+3,4,d+4 (i27) and CS.1+2 (i32). The rest you block on a guess. Most recover crouching (FC or c): punish with a WS launcher. TekkenDocs gives high-crush on d+2, db+1, db+3, db+4, 1,4 (14~), CS.3 (7~40) and CS.1+2 (1~33), so a high jab whiffs into them.

All his Deathfists get stronger (Wavu and anti-strat). Heat makes some moves safer, so check the numbers: CS.2 −17 → H.CS.2 −13 · 1+2 −10 → H.1+2 −9 · d+1,2 −17 → H.d+1,2 −13. Practice priorities

1. SSR in neutral and at −1 to −5. Block, don't step, when you're −3 or worse and expect df+1, d+1 or 3.
2. Block Demoman standing after the low. d+4 (−31c), d+4,2 (−18) and d+4,2,1+2 (−17) are all launch punishes. It only lands at point-blank range.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt his plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a startup range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered. Ground (g) and knockdown (d) hit advantages are left out.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. Wavu says otherwise for DPD (cancel to block from 10f) and for CS (from 24f); no source says Paul can block in Sway.
CS doesn't apply: no CS entry has a stated frame advantage (the 1+2~f cancel has none), so there is no CS table.
State changes the read (Wavu/TekkenDocs): a standing jab is a high. DPD is crouching 1~30, so the jab whiffs there whatever the numbers say. Sway from an attack has high evasion, and SWA.3 is crouching from frame 1, so the jab can whiff there too. SWA.1+2 has its own high evasion. SWA.4 is homing. No move from either stance is a power crush.

Lab drill — training wheels: have the dummy throw the standalone opener, then step.
