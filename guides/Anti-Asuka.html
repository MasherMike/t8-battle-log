Movement — Which Way to Step↑ Return to Top

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR) in neutral. The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written) says "Generally SSR. SSL very strong after f4 (+3) because of axis." The @Mezerdie sidestep chart (via sidestep.md) also says SSR. Wavu's per-move notes split evenly between the two sides and don't name a default.

Exceptions:
• f,F+2 (i15~16 mid, her dash elbow): Wavu — "Has good right sidestep tracking." It catches your SSR. It's −9 on block, so just block it.
• After f+4 (+3 on block): step LEFT. The anti-strat says SSL is "very strong" there because of the axis. f+4 is a homing high, so you can also duck it before it lands.
• SSL beats three of her strings (Wavu): 2,1,d+1+2 and b+3,2 (from 1st block), and the charged f,F+2,3* (SSL or either sidewalk, from 1st block).
• When she's plus (f,F+1 is +5): the anti-strat says stepping gets inconsistent and you "have to walk stuff".
• Rage Art: per the anti-strat, immediate and medium holds of R.df+1+2 track your SS/SW. Only the max hold (−13) is steppable.
• Homing (list below): f+4, f+1,4, df+3+4, f,F+3, f,f,F+1+2 (and its NWG version) and both generic throws.

So the habit is: SSR her neutral pokes and block f,F+2. Switch to SSL after a blocked f+4 and against her 2,1 and b+3 strings. Walk when she's +5. Block or duck the homing moves.

Stance Counterplay↑ Return to Top

DST — Destabilizer (command grab stance)

• Entry: f+1+3 (alt. f+2+4), a grab, i19~20, +17 on hit (Wavu). Also ws2,1+2 on hit (+17, "Links to Destabilizer extensions on hit"), −5 on block. The anti-strat calls f1+3 an "i19 unbreakable grab"; Wavu lists no break.
• No input = −3 (Wavu: "−3 when exiting stance with no input").
• Can she block in it? No source says she can. By the guide's rule she can't block in a unique stance.
• Lows: only DST.4: i19, −14 on block Not reactable.
• Mid: DST.2: i19, −3, Heat Engager.
• No parry and no power crush from DST.
• The answer: it's a three-way guess after the grab connects (mid, low, or nothing). See Stance Check for the arithmetic.

Not a held stance, but two moves come out of a sidestep. SS.2 (mid, i13~14, −13) is a Heat Engager and punishable. SS.4 (high, i17~21, −6~−2) can be ducked.

NWG — Naniwa Gusto (power-up state)

Not a stance, but it changes her frames. She gains it on hit with several moves (f+2, b+3,2, b+4,2,3 and db+1+2 with 1; f+3+4; f,F+2,1+2), from the Heat Smash, and always during Heat (anti-strat: "Naniwa Gusto always activated"). NWG versions are much safer: NWG.u+1+2 +8, NWG.f,f,F+1+2 +7, NWG.f,F+1+2 +2, uf+1,NWG.2 ±0. Each one consumes NWG and costs Heat time (see Heat).

Heat↑ Return to Top

Heat Smash H.2+3: mid → attack throw, i16, +10 on block (Wavu +10g). No punish. Don't press. It becomes mid, mid, mid on block or whiff, and she's airborne from frame 5. Gives her NWG.
Heat Burst 2+3: mid, i16, power crush 7~16, +1 on block.
Heat power: Naniwa Gusto is always active in Heat (anti-strat).
Theory (Ledger): her Heat Smash is airborne from frame 5, so a low or throw you're already pressing when she activates most likely whiffs under it. Block, don't low-check. The cutoff (does an i12 low land before frame 5?) is in the To-test list.

General Counter Play↑ Return to Top

Practice priorities

1. SSR her neutral pokes, but block f,F+2 (tracks SSR, −9) and SSL after a blocked f+4 (+3).
2. Duck her highs: the f+1+2 power crush, f+4, f,f,F+1+2, and the high enders of db+1,2 and f,F+2,1 (both Heat Engagers).
3. Launch her slow homing lows: df+3+4 and f+1,4 (both −26, reactable). Don't press into b+1 (hit-throw on CH).
4. Punish the big mids: b+3 −19, b+3,2 −20, f+2 −18, 2,3 −17, b+4,2,3 −16, ws3 −16, db+1,4 −15. Watch for the D cancels on f+2 and b+3,2.
5. After the Destabilizer grab: it's a guess. Crouch is the higher-reward guess (DST.4 is −14).

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt her plus stance entries?↑ Return to Top

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = her raw startup − her frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range, the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus advantage are covered. Knockdown advantages (ub+1+2~D, +6d) are left out.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. No source says Asuka can block in DST. FC is her ordinary full crouch, not a unique stance.
State changes the read (Wavu/TekkenDocs): a standing jab is a high, so it whiffs against everything from FC (hFC moves are crouching from frame 1). hFC.3+4 is airborne 5~19. None of the DST or FC moves is a power crush.

Lab drill — training wheels: have the dummy throw the standalone poke, then step. Stage 1 (direction only, timing untested): the dummy plays db+1 on fixed timing from neutral, and you SSR it. 20-rep sets, graduate at 18/20 on 3 straight sets (about 60 reps minimum). Stage 2: random playback of db+1, df+1 and 2 at randomized timing, 16/20 on 3 straight sets. Stage 3: mix in f,F+2 (tracks SSR, so block it, −9) and f+4 (homing high, so duck it, or block and SSL next), 25-rep sets, 75% correct decisions on 2 straight sets. Stage 4: log what holds in real matches as Lab (Mike). Why Stage 3 matters for her: her tracking splits (f,F+2 right, uf+1 left) and her step side flips after f+4, so SSR has to become a read, not a reflex. Weaknesses: a standalone poke has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
